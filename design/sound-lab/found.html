Coin and card sounds, short-listed

Coins: clicky but metallic (a light natural ring allowed), three amounts plus all-in. Cards: smooth, not sharp, for dealt, played onto the pile, turned face-up, folded and collected. Up to six candidates per slot from our licensed library and the whole ArtOrDie paper-card recording. Press a tile to hear it.

How these were chosen. I can't hear, so every card, chip and coin recording was split into single sound events and measured. Anything that breaks your brief was dropped: for cards, sharp attacks (under 6 ms to full loudness), very bright snaps and zip-like sweeps; for coins, the wrong number of hits for the amount, or more than a light ring. The coin sounds were then ranked by CLAP, a local sound–text model, against the descriptions shown under each slot. CLAP can't tell card actions apart (it calls nearly every card sound "flipped"), so each card slot was filled by shape instead, with CLAP only confirming it sounds like cards. No sound appears in more than one slot. CLAP only pre-sorts: in its blind test it named the right sound first 11 times in 19, and it's weakest on single card landings. Your ears decide. Each tile shows: where it came from (its library label) · CLAP margin (how much better it matches the slot's descriptions than unrelated sounds like a pen click, glass or a door; higher is surer, and only sounds above 0 are listed) · number of hits · length · attack (how fast it reaches full loudness: longer is smoother) · brightness · ring (pitch share; above 0.40 rings a little).
