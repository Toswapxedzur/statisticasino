Your spec: the 52-card stack turns over side-over, like a page, lifted and flipped in place, with a random card face on the bottom, in about 0.9 s. The motion is the minimum-jerk curve, the standard model of a hand-driven move: θ(t) = π(10τ³ − 15τ⁴ + 6τ⁵). Speed peaks at 6.5 rad/s (375°/s) halfway through; acceleration and deceleration are mirror images, so the deck spins up and settles without a jolt. The physics uses a real deck: 94 g, 63.5 mm wide, 17 mm thick, spinning about its own long axis: I = m(W² + T²)/12 = 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ kg·m². Angular momentum and torque follow from ω and α. The lift rises and falls on a smooth bump, 37 mm at its peak, sized so the deck's lowest corner clears the table (about 5 mm at the tightest point) and it lands exactly where it started.
