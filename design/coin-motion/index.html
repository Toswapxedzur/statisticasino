Bluffing Valley · currency animation · demo

Coins that add up like digits

Each player's coins sit right beside their badge, on the side facing the table, and read like a number: the most valuable coin next to the badge, the smallest farthest out, then the amount. A bet's coins land on their own-value column. When a column fills up (5 coppers, 4 golds…) those coins merge into one bigger coin, which steps toward the badge. When the street ends, every column flies to the pot's same-value column and the bet numbers ride into the pot's number. Splitting a pot or returning a bet breaks a bigger coin into smaller ones first, like borrowing in subtraction.

The coins are a number: 1 · 5 · 25 · 100 · 500 · 2.5K · 10K · 50K · 250K · 1M · 5M, each worth 5, 5, then 4 of the one below (repeating). A pile never holds a full set of any coin: it merges them up first, so every pile reads as one proper number. Timing: a bet's columns leave 40 ms apart and land 0.38 s later · merges wait a 0.06 s beat, then take 0.26 s each (chains follow one another) · the sweep waits for the last bet and its merges, then takes 0.52 s, all piles landing at once · a break takes 0.26 s · the win flies 0.6 s, sinks 0.22 s, and the number counts up over 0.52 s. Motion: every move is minimum-jerk, like the cards: speed and acceleration are zero at both ends. Coins lift a little in flight, and a merge or break hops over its neighbour; there's no bounce. Columns keep the 60° zig-zag, at the table's real coin size. Sound: the game's own sounds, played when coins land: a chip push per bet, one pot sound per sweep, the win sound as the winnings land, a knock on a check. A checked-through street makes no chip sound.
